Reduction Gluer

Second insem, Question 1. Glue points of the frame together yourself and the page checks, point by point, whether the result is a reduction.

The frame you are gluing

The frame has five points a, b, c, d, e. Every point sees itself. The printed arrows are a to b, a to c, b to d, c to d, b to e. That relation is not transitive (a sees b and b sees d, yet (a,d) is missing), and an intuitionistic frame must be a partial order, so we add the missing arrows (a,d) and (a,e). This is the one assumption to write in your exam answer.

The one rule

Gluing points means they turn into a single point. A single point has a single future, so only points with the same future can be glued. To test it: write down x↑, rename every point in it to the name of its glued group, and compare that with what the glued point of x sees. The two lists must be identical, for every x.

"Glued point sees something x does not" breaks rule R2 (never gain a view). "x sees something the glued point does not" breaks rule R1 (never lose a view). Matching lists mean both rules hold. The glued frame must also stay a partial order: two different glued points may not see each other.

What happens to every arrow

Each point is replaced by the name of its group. An arrow between two points of the same group becomes the loop of the glued point. Everything else becomes an arrow between group names, and duplicates collapse into one.

Exam recipe for any "find all reducts" question

  1. Write the relation as a list of up-sets x↑. If the given relation is not transitive, close it and say so.
  2. Mark the final points (x↑ has one element) and the root.
  3. Decide from the top down. First choose which final points are glued, because everything below is judged by the groups above it. Here that is two cases: d and e glued, or not.
  4. For each lower point, compare futures after renaming. Two points can join only if their renamed up-sets are the same, which may force other gluings first (a and b together force c and d together).
  5. Write each surviving grouping, then draw it: one node per group, an arrow when any member sees any member, then delete loops and implied arrows.
  6. Collect identical drawings. State the final list and one line of reason for the ones that fail.
